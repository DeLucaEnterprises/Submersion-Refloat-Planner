    How the simulation works

    Body. Each simulated body gets a net weight in water from the buoyancy data above. That weight is then adjusted for body weight, body fat (fat 0.90 kg/L, lean tissue 1.10 kg/L, baseline 20%), salt water, and the water that replaces lung air on drowning (0.5–1.5 kg). Clothing adds wet weight and air that compresses with depth (Boyle's law).

    Descent. The body sinks at terminal velocity, recalculated every 0.5 ft as trapped air compresses (tumbling drag area 0.30–0.60 m²). Before sinking, the surface phase depends on the scenario: about 45 s to aspirate when unconscious; distress plus the 20–60 s instinctive response when unable to swim; time to swim failure, then that response, for an exhausted swimmer.

    Resting position. Surface drift plus a 3–20° glide cone on the way down. A swimmer moves along the shore at 0.25–0.6 m/s with frequent reversals, limited by the shoreline length.

    Refloat. Putrefactive gas is produced in a pulse whose speed scales with temperature (Q10 2.5–4, tapering to zero between 46 °F and 39 °F). Gas leaks away at 5–20% per day, faster under pressure. The body rises when retained gas exceeds its negative buoyancy multiplied by the absolute pressure at depth. All parameter ranges are sampled per body. The tool reports percentages of simulated bodies.

    Updating the odds by zone. Each zone's starting weight is multiplied by the chance a body there would still be down after the observed number of days, then normalized. This is standard Bayesian search updating, using the model's refloat curve as the evidence.

    Limits: when not to rely on this

    Still water only. Currents, river flow, tides, waves and wind drift are not modeled, so do not use this for moving water.

    Water temperature is held constant at the resting depth. In shallow water, seasonal warming or cooling will shift the result. Rerun with the temperature expected over the coming weeks.

    Not modeled: entanglement on debris, injuries that vent gas, scavenging, intoxication, medications, and a life jacket. Each of these changes the outcome; most make refloat less likely.

    The swim-failure time for a clothed person at night and the gas capacity of a body are assumptions calibrated to published benchmarks, not direct measurements. Treat outputs as planning ranges. This tool supports a search plan; it does not replace it.

    Decision-support model for search planning. Outputs are percentages of simulated bodies, not predictions for any individual. Built from published data on buoyancy, drowning, swim failure and aquatic decomposition (see Evidence). Research basis: The Find Me Project modeling study, October 2026.
